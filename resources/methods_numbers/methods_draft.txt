Computational methods
Datasets
All data in this study are Slide-tags single-nucleus RNA-seq of tissue sections (Russell et al. 2024; see the experimental methods). In Slide-tags, a fresh-frozen section is placed on an array of DNA-barcoded beads whose positions are known from sequencing of the array. Spatial barcodes released from the beads diffuse into the section and tag the nuclei, which are then dissociated from the tissue and profiled in droplets (10x Genomics Chromium). Each 10x lane yields two sequencing libraries, a gene-expression library and a spatial-barcode library, and every read carries the barcode of the droplet it came from (the cell barcode) and a unique molecular identifier (UMI). A nucleus is therefore a droplet barcode called as containing a nucleus (by CellBender; below), not an object segmented from an image: gene-expression reads are assigned to nuclei through their cell barcode and counted as UMIs per gene, and a nucleus's position is estimated from the beads whose spatial barcodes it captured (below). Nuclei whose position could not be estimated keep their expression profile and enter all non-spatial analyses.
The mouse dataset (GsD X-Mens) comprises nine 10x lanes from six arrays, one per timepoint (uninduced; 1.5 dpi early; 1.5 dpi late; 2.5 dpi; 3.5 dpi; 4.5 dpi bleeding), with the nuclei of each array split across one or two lanes. Each array carried one uterine cross-section, except the uninduced array, which carried two small consecutive sections side by side. The human dataset comprises six lanes from two endometrial sections from two donors, one in the secretory and one in the menstrual phase, with three lanes per section. After quality control and annotation (below), the data comprise 57,015 mouse nuclei (3,142 to 16,569 per array) and 67,792 human nuclei (31,766 secretory and 36,081 menstrual), of which 29,678 and 46,470 have a position after spatial quality control. Count matrices of all annotated nuclei are available as described under Reproducibility, code and data availability.
Alignment, quantification and ambient RNA removal
Raw reads of both libraries were demultiplexed with Cell Ranger mkfastq (8.0.1), and gene-expression reads were aligned and quantified with the 10x Genomics Cell Ranger count pipeline (version 7.2.0) with default parameters. Human libraries were aligned to the 10x GRCh38 2024-A reference transcriptome. Mouse libraries were aligned to a custom reference built with Cell Ranger mkref (7.2.0) from the GRCm38 (mm10) genome and GENCODE vM23 (Ensembl 98) annotation, augmented with the DREADD transgene sequences (Gq-DREADD and Gs-DREADD). Ambient RNA was removed from the raw count matrices with CellBender remove-background (Fleming et al. 2023; version 0.2.2; default 150 epochs, CUDA enabled) per lane. Spatial-barcode reads were processed with the Slide-tags pipeline (Russell et al. 2024; https://github.com/thechenlab/Slide-tags). Reads with an N in the UMI, without a detectable universal primer site (allowing one deletion or up to two mismatches) or with a spatial barcode absent from the array's barcode list (allowing one mismatch or deletion) were discarded, as were chimeric reads, reads with low-quality spatial barcodes and reads whose cell barcode was not called as a nucleus. The remaining reads were collapsed into UMI counts per nucleus and spatial barcode. For each nucleus, DBSCAN (Ester et al. 1996) classified its spatial barcodes, placed at their beads' array coordinates, as signal or noise, with ε and minPts optimized automatically; a nucleus with a single signal cluster was placed at the UMI-weighted centroid of that cluster, and other nuclei received no position.
Quality control
Unless otherwise noted, quality control and preprocessing used Scanpy (version 1.11.5; Wolf et al. 2018) and anndata (0.12.11; Virshup et al. 2024). Per sample, nuclei with fewer than 500 CellBender-corrected UMIs were excluded. Following the single-cell best practice recommendations (Heumos et al. 2023), nuclei in which mitochondrial genes contributed more than 8% of counts were removed, as were nuclei whose log10-transformed total counts or log10-transformed number of detected genes lay more than 3 median absolute deviations (MADs) from the sample median (Supplementary Table S5 lists the resulting per-sample cutoffs).
Doublets were identified per sample with DoubletDetection (version 4.2; Gayoso et al. 2018), which performs Leiden clustering and selection of 3,000 highly variable genes internally. A boosting classifier was run for 40 iterations with a p-value threshold of 10−7 and a voter threshold of 0.5, convergence of the doublet count across iterations was inspected, and predicted doublets were removed. A second, cluster-level exclusion applied during annotation is described below. Of 90,456 mouse and 97,470 human CellBender-called nuclei, the UMI floor removed 7,460 and 4,948, the mitochondrial filter 0 and 7, the count-depth MAD filter 2,211 and 5,474, the detected-gene MAD filter 1,219 and 1,731, and the doublet classifier 11,497 and 14,947, leaving 68,069 mouse and 70,363 human nuclei for integration (filters applied in that order; per-sample counts in Supplementary Table S5).
Dimensionality reduction, clustering and annotation
Samples of each species were concatenated into one AnnData object per species (Virshup et al. 2024). For each object, 3,000 highly variable genes were selected with scanpy.pp.highly_variable_genes (flavor seurat_v3, on raw counts, sample as batch key) after removing mitochondrial and ribosomal genes from the candidate pool. An scVI variational autoencoder (Lopez et al. 2018; scvi-tools 1.4.2, Gayoso et al. 2022; PyTorch 2.11.0) was trained with two encoder/decoder layers, 30 latent dimensions and a negative-binomial likelihood on the raw counts of these genes with sample as batch key, for the default number of epochs with early stopping and the evidence lower bound monitored for convergence (seed 0).
A k-nearest-neighbor graph (k = 100) was built on the 30-dimensional latent space. Leiden clustering (Traag et al. 2019; scanpy.tl.leiden with the igraph implementation, Csardi and Nepusz 2006; random seed 0) was run at 21 resolutions from 0.1 to 10, and a UMAP (McInnes et al. 2018; scanpy.tl.umap; min_dist 0.5, spread 1.0) was computed on the same graph.
Major cell classes were annotated by manual curation at Leiden resolution 2.5, deliberately over-clustering relative to the expected number of classes so that populations co-expressing markers of more than one class resolved as discrete clusters (38 clusters for 6 classes in mouse, 34 for 5 in human). Each cluster was assigned to a class (stromal, epithelial, immune, endothelial, smooth muscle and, in mouse only, perimetrial) from canonical marker genes of published mouse and human endometrial single-cell datasets (Kirkwood et al. 2021, 2022; Wang et al. 2020a; Winkler et al. 2024), examined with scanpy.pl.dotplot. In mouse, the markers were Cdh1 and Pax8 (epithelial), Pdgfra (stromal, with Wnt4, Prl8a2, Bmp2 and Cdh3 marking decidual cells), Flt1 and Pecam1 (endothelial), Ptprc (immune), Acta2 and Myh11 (smooth muscle) and Sox6 (perimetrial); in human, EPCAM and KLF5 (epithelial), COL5A1, COL6A3 and DCN (stromal, with LEFTY2 and IGFBP1 marking decidual cells), PECAM1 and VWF (endothelial), PTPRC and STK17B (immune) and ACTA2 and MYH11 (smooth muscle). The progesterone and estrogen receptors (Pgr and Esr1 in mouse, PGR and ESR1 in human) were displayed for context but not used for assignment, because they are expressed in several classes. Upon manual marker review, 9 of 38 clusters (9,715 nuclei) were excluded in mouse, leaving 58,354, and 3 of 34 clusters (1,217 nuclei) in human, leaving 69,146. Four of these clusters co-expressed markers of two classes (3 in mouse, 3,494 nuclei; 1 in human, 1,137 nuclei) and had high doublet scores, although every nucleus had passed the per-nucleus doublet filter (mean DoubletDetection score 8.24 versus 1.60 in retained nuclei in mouse; 11.16 versus 1.51 in human). Two mouse clusters (4,511 nuclei) had low RNA content (median 859 detected genes per nucleus, versus 1,793 in retained nuclei) and came mostly from a single array each (89% of one from an uninduced array, 80% of the other from a 3.5 dpi array). One mouse cluster (15 nuclei) consisted of neurons, and one human cluster had only 4 nuclei. The remaining four clusters were identified on later review as lymphatic endothelial cells (535 nuclei), pericytes (1,043) and plasma cells (117) in mouse and B cells (76) in human (Table M1); they were not carried into the class-level analyses, but they are labeled in the extended objects and were included in the whole-tissue embedding (below).
Retained nuclei were split by major cell class, and each was re-embedded and integrated with the same scVI procedure (3,000 highly variable genes re-selected on the subset with the seurat_v3 method, after removing genes detected in fewer than 3 nuclei; the selection pooled all samples rather than fitting per sample, because per-sample variance fits are unstable in small classes). Within each class, a 100-nearest-neighbor graph was built on the scVI latent space and clustered at Leiden resolution 3.0, again deliberately over-clustering relative to the expected number of cell types (178 clusters for 25 fine cell types in mouse, 179 for 17 in human; Supplementary Table S6). Clusters were merged into fine cell types or excluded on marker review. The 14 mouse and 11 human clusters excluded removed a further 1,339 mouse and 1,354 human nuclei, leaving 57,015 mouse and 67,792 human annotated nuclei. Marker genes of each cell type are listed in Table M1.

Human epithelial nuclei were assigned to an inner or an outer layer by their position in the tissue. On the sections, most clusters of the epithelial embedding (Leiden resolution 3.0) lay predominantly either among decidualized stroma lining the uterine cavity (labeled inner) or among endometrial-fibroblast-rich stroma toward the myometrium (labeled outer). For every spatially placed epithelial nucleus, decidual cells and endometrial fibroblasts were counted among its 20 nearest placed nuclei of the same section; the nucleus was assigned to the inner layer when decidual cells were more numerous and to the outer layer when fibroblasts were, and nuclei with equal counts (221 of 8,548, 2.6%) were not used. Each cluster was labeled with the layer of the majority of its placed nuclei (median 88.0% of a cluster's placed nuclei; range 52.8–99.8%), giving 24 inner clusters (5,539 nuclei) and 25 outer clusters (6,086 nuclei). Ciliated cells (one cluster, 91 nuclei) kept their own label. Three clusters (1,017 nuclei) that had been excluded because PECAM1 was detected in 18–38% of their nuclei were retained as inner epithelium: they expressed PAX8 (74–92% of nuclei) and PAEP (49–96%) but no other endothelial transcripts (VWF in at most 2% and CDH5 in at most 1% of nuclei), and 94.8–99.8% of their placed nuclei lay in the inner layer. Genes distinguishing the layers were identified by comparing inner with outer nuclei within each donor (two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test with tie correction on counts normalized to 10,000 per nucleus and log-transformed; scanpy.tl.rank_genes_groups; Benjamini–Hochberg correction): in both donors, 676 genes were at least two-fold higher in inner nuclei and 108 in outer nuclei (adjusted P < 0.05; Supplementary Table S7). Ranked by the smaller of the two donors' differences in the fraction of nuclei detecting the gene, the protein-coding genes most consistently higher in inner nuclei were DST, TANC2, ABCA1, SVIL, SOX4, SKIL and RDH10, and MAP2 was nearly confined to inner nuclei (29–40% of inner vs 4% of outer nuclei); outer nuclei were marked by CTNNA2 (91–95% of outer vs 47–65% of inner nuclei) and ST3GAL5.
Mouse epithelial nuclei were assigned to luminal or glandular epithelium by the same approach. Lumen-side stroma comprised subluminal fibroblasts (eFib3) and decidual cells, which fill the subluminal stroma after induction, and deeper stroma the other endometrial fibroblasts (eFib1, eFib2 and eFib2i). A spatially placed epithelial nucleus counted as luminal when lumen-side stromal nuclei outnumbered deeper fibroblasts among its 20 nearest placed nuclei of the same section and as glandular for the reverse; nuclei with equal counts (68 of 2,301) were not used. Each cluster of the epithelial embedding (Leiden resolution 3.0) took the compartment of the majority of its placed nuclei (median 89.8%; range 50.7–97.2%), giving 12 luminal clusters (2,830 nuclei) and 12 glandular clusters (2,985 nuclei); 87.9% of placed nuclei lay in the compartment of their cluster's label. Two clusters (432 nuclei, 389 from induced uteri) were labeled proliferative epithelium without a compartment: 63.7% and 72.2% of their nuclei were in S or G2/M phase (scanpy.tl.score_genes_cell_cycle on all 6,681 nuclei of the epithelial lineage, with the S and G2/M gene sets of Tirosh et al. 2016 and the symbol updates of Seurat's cc.genes.updated.2019), against at most 43.9% in any luminal or glandular cluster, and 1,817 genes were at least two-fold higher in their 389 induced nuclei than in the 1,003 other epithelial nuclei of induced uteri (adjusted P < 0.05). Luminal and glandular nuclei were compared as for the human layers, separately within uninduced uteri (2,549 luminal and 2,263 glandular nuclei) and induced uteri (281 and 722): 353 genes were at least two-fold higher in luminal and 783 in glandular nuclei in both (adjusted P < 0.05; Supplementary Table S8), including Wnt7a among the luminal and Foxa2 among the glandular genes.
Finally, the annotated nuclei and the four populations set aside above were re-integrated into whole-tissue atlases with scVI as above (Lopez et al. 2018). For UMAP visualization, a handful of cells whose neighborhoods were inconsistent with their lineage label (at most 20% of 30 nearest neighbors sharing the nucleus’s lineage and at least 70% from one other lineage, in either the scVI latent space or the UMAP) were flagged as embedding outliers, which were omitted from UMAP plots but retained for all other analyses: 34 of 57,033 mouse nuclei (21 of 43,197 fibroblast/decidual) and 25 of 67,847 human nuclei (9 of 47,533 fibroblast/decidual). The comparison with Ulrich et al. and the human-to-mouse transfer (SAMap) use all 47,533 human fibroblast/decidual nuclei, including any flagged as embedding outliers; flagged nuclei are left out only of panels that draw individual human nuclei (the stromal UMAPs and tissue maps).
Spatial quality control
Spatial coordinates were quality-controlled after annotation. Positions failing a criterion were set to missing, though the nuclei themselves were retained for all non-spatial analyses. First, within each 10x lane, a position was blanked (set to NaN) if it duplicated another nucleus’s coordinate or if its mean distance to its five nearest positioned neighbors was below 10 µm. Second, the mean distance to the five nearest neighbors was recomputed among the remaining positions of each timepoint (lanes pooled), and a position was blanked if log(1 + distance) exceeded the timepoint median by more than 5 median absolute deviations (unscaled). Third, positions outside the tissue sections that the distance criterion missed were blanked with ten rectangular exclusion regions drawn by hand on plots of the raw coordinates (5 in the uninduced sample, 3 at 3.5 dpi, and 1 each at 1.5 dpi early and 2.5 dpi; listed in the workflow configuration). In mouse, the three steps blanked 1,272, 544 and 136 positions, respectively (the exclusion regions covered 112 nuclei in the uninduced sample, 12 at 3.5 dpi, 7 at 1.5 dpi early and 5 at 2.5 dpi), leaving 29,678 of 57,033 nuclei with a position. A further 25,403 had no position from Slide-tags. For the human data, only the first step was applied (4 positions blanked). In total, 46,470 of 67,847 human nuclei have a position, and 21,373 had none from Slide-tags. In all spatial figures, nuclei from 10x lanes of one timepoint (sections of the same tissue in one coordinate frame) are pooled, and mouse sections are rotated rigidly about the median position of their plotted nuclei for display.
Pseudotime
We ordered endometrial fibroblasts and decidual cells by diffusion pseudotime (DPT) computed on a time-informed transition matrix, using one procedure for both species. In mouse, the 907 apoptotic decidual nuclei were excluded from the pseudotime computation and carry no pseudotime value. Transitions between consecutive timepoints were estimated by unbalanced entropic optimal transport with moscot (v0.5.0; Klein et al. 2025; TemporalProblem): squared Euclidean cost in each species' 30-dimensional stromal scVI latent space, scaled by its mean; ε = 10−3; τa = τb = 0.95; prior growth rates from moscot's proliferation and apoptosis gene sets. Both marginals were relaxed because the sampled populations are not closed: each timepoint is a separate tissue sample, stromal cells proliferate and die during decidualization, and apoptotic decidual nuclei were excluded. All couplings converged. The couplings were combined in a CellRank RealTimeKernel (v2.0.7; Weiler et al. 2024) with the kernel settings of the CellRank protocol (Weiler and Theis 2026, Procedure 4; self_transitions = "all", conn_weight = 0.2, threshold = "auto"). Each row thus assigns 80% of its probability to the nucleus's row-normalized coupling to the next timepoint and 20% to a within-timepoint 15-nearest-neighbor graph (Scanpy defaults: 50-component PCA of log-normalized expression of the 3,000 stromal highly variable genes, computed per timepoint). Nuclei at the last timepoint use the within-timepoint graph only. Following the real-time-informed pseudotime of Weiler et al. (2024), we symmetrized the transition matrix ((T + TT)/2, rows renormalized) and computed a 15-component diffusion map (Coifman et al. 2005). The root was the earliest-timepoint fibroblast at the fibroblast end of DC1, the component most correlated with the annotated fibroblast-to-decidual order (Spearman ρ = 0.89 mouse, 0.72 human): an uninduced eFib1 nucleus in mouse (ACCCTTGAGCATGAAT-1-uninduced_mck8b) and a secretory-phase fibroblast in human (GAGACCCAGAATAGTC-1-secretory_mck6b). DPT (Haghverdi et al. 2016) was computed with scanpy.tl.dpt on all 15 components and rank-normalized (average rank / N) for all downstream analyses. Mouse pseudotime increased with subtype order (ρ = 0.86) and timepoint (ρ = 0.62). Human fibroblasts preceded decidual cells (median 0.28 vs 0.75; ρ = 0.70), and pseudotime increased from the secretory to the menstrual phase (ρ = 0.34).
Pseudotime-associated genes and cross-species conservation
Genes associated with pseudotime were identified with a mutual information (MI)-based approach adapted from previous studies of the menstrual and mouse estrous cycles (Wang et al. 2020a; Winkler et al. 2024). All genes detected in at least 1% of nuclei were tested (14,261 mouse and 15,008 human genes). For each such gene, MI between log-normalized expression and pseudotime was estimated on all nuclei with the k-nearest-neighbor estimator implemented in sklearn.feature_selection.mutual_info_regression (scikit-learn 1.8.0, Pedregosa et al. 2011; Kraskov et al. 2004). Significance was assessed by permuting pseudotime 1,000 times. Genes were processed in 200 chunks; all genes in a chunk shared the same 1,000 permutations. The one-sided empirical p-value was (number of permutations with MI ≥ observed + 1)/(1,000 + 1). P-values were adjusted with the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995; statsmodels 0.14.6), and genes with q < 0.05 were considered significant (14,261 mouse and 14,921 human genes). Given the permutation resolution and the pervasive association of expression with differentiation pseudotime, significance served as a floor excluding estimator noise rather than a strict selective criterion. Top pseudotime-associated genes were defined as those whose MI exceeded the 90th percentile among significant genes in each species: 1,426 mouse genes (MI > 0.070 nats) and 1,492 human genes (MI > 0.028 nats) (Supplementary Fig. S29A-B).
Strict one-to-one human-mouse orthologs were obtained from the Ensembl Compara release 116 human-mouse homologies of protein-coding genes (Harrison et al. 2024; homology type ortholog_one2one; 16,275 gene pairs). The background for the comparison comprised the 9,606 one-to-one pairs in which both genes were detected in at least 1% of fibroblasts and decidual cells and significant for MI (q < 0.05) in their species, and the top gene sets were restricted to this background (1,132 mouse genes, K; 1,191 human genes, n). Their overlap (k = 371 pairs) was tested with a hypergeometric test (scipy.stats.hypergeom in SciPy, Virtanen et al. 2020; N = 9,606, computed on the log scale with hypergeom.logsf): the expected overlap was K × n / N = 140.4 genes (12% of the human top set) and the observed overlap 31% (2.64-fold enrichment; p = 1.0 × 10−84). To check that the result did not depend on the 90th-percentile threshold, the test was repeated at every percentile cutoff from the 40th to the 99th, recording −log10 p, fold enrichment and the 2.5th-97.5th percentile range of the hypergeometric null (Supplementary Fig. S29C-D).
Pseudotime expression heatmaps
To compare gene expression changes during induced decidualization to pregnancy in Yang et al. (2023), nuclei subsetted from induced timepoints were ordered by mouse pseudotime and re-ranked within this subset. Smoothed profiles of the 28 panel genes were computed with a reimplementation of Monocle 2's plot_pseudotime_heatmap (Trapnell et al. 2014; Qiu et al. 2017). For each gene, a negative-binomial generalized linear model (VGAM; Yee 2015) of size-factor-normalized UMI counts was fitted on a natural cubic spline of pseudotime (3 degrees of freedom). Size factors were library size divided by the geometric mean library size over all nuclei. Each gene's dispersion was taken from Monocle's parametric dispersion-mean trend (dispersion = a + b/mean), fitted across all 27,064 genes detected in more than one nucleus. Fitted values at 100 evenly spaced points along pseudotime (equal numbers of nuclei per column) were log10(x + 1)-transformed, z-scored per gene and clipped to [−3, 3]. Rows follow the gene order of Yang et al. (2023) and were not clustered.
For cross-species pseudotime heatmaps, candidate genes were the 273 strict one-to-one orthologs ranked in the top 10% by MI with pseudotime in both species (MI ranked among the 9,638 orthologs tested in both; a stricter subset of the 371 shared top genes above). For each, we computed the Pearson correlation between the human and mouse expression profiles along pseudotime (mean log-normalized expression in 50 equal-count pseudotime bins, z-scored per gene). Among the 98 genes with r > 0.8, genes were classified as early or late by the pseudotime centroid of their profile (mean bin position on a 0–1 scale, weighted by the positive part of the z-scored profile and averaged over the two species; < 0.4 or > 0.6), and the 14 most concordant early and 14 most concordant late genes were displayed (r = 0.88-0.99), ordered by centroid. Smoothed profiles were computed separately for each species on all fibroblasts and decidual nuclei (42,290 mouse, 47,533 human) along that species' rank-normalized pseudotime, with the Monocle 2 procedure above (size factors and dispersion trend computed within each species; the human trend across 31,735 genes detected in more than one nucleus), then log10(x + 1)-transformed, z-scored per gene within each species and clipped to [−3, 3].
Cross-species alignment and transfer of categorical and continuous values (SAMap)
SAMap builds its cross-species gene graph from all homologous gene pairs rather than from a list of orthologs: links between sequence-similar genes, including paralogs and one-to-many relationships, are initialized from sequence similarity and reweighted by expression correlation as the alignment is refined, so restricting the input to one-to-one orthologs would remove the correspondences SAMap is designed to resolve (Tarashansky et al. 2021). Human and mouse proteomes (Ensembl release 113; GRCh38, GRCm39) were searched reciprocally with phmmer (HMMER 3.4; Eddy 2011) at E ≤ 10−5. Because the proteomes include all annotated isoforms, hits were collapsed to gene symbols using the gene_symbol field of each FASTA header, retaining the highest-scoring isoform pair per ordered symbol pair. Proteins lacking a gene symbol were excluded. When building its homology graph, SAMap retained only pairs with E ≤ 10−6 (its default threshold).
Fibroblast and decidual nuclei from all timepoints were supplied to SAMap (sc-samap 3.0.1; Tarashansky et al. 2021) as raw integer counts indexed by gene symbol. Per-species manifolds were built with SAMap’s internal settings and stitched over the phmmer tables for three iterations, with 20 cross-species neighbors per nucleus and edge weights rescaled by cross-species expression correlation. Compartment correspondence is reported as the share of the source compartment’s summed cross-species edge weight that lands in each compartment of the other species (share landing in the matching compartment: 88% for human fibroblasts, 82% for human decidual cells, 85% for mouse fibroblasts and 92% for mouse decidual cells; mean total cross-species edge weight 12.6-13.5 of a maximum of 20 in all four compartments). The corresponding SAMap alignment scores were 0.58 for both matched compartments and 0.08 and 0.12 off-diagonal.
Values were transferred between species with a single kernel-weighted average, using the SAMap cross-species edge weights of each receiving nucleus as the kernel (a Nadaraya-Watson-type linear smoother; Hastie et al. 2009; Nadaraya 1964; Watson 1964). For a receiving nucleus i in one species, with w_ij the edge weight to nucleus j of the other species, the normalized weights are a_ij = w_ij / Σ_(k∈S) w_ik, where S is the set of cross-species neighbors of i that carry the quantity being transferred. A continuous value v is transferred as its weighted mean, v̂_i = Σ_(j∈S) a_ij v_j. A categorical value y is transferred as the weighted share of each category c, p̂_ic = Σ_(j∈S) a_ij 1[y_j = c]; the shares of a nucleus sum to 1, and where a single label is needed it is the category with the largest share, argmax_c p̂_ic. The weights are non-negative and derived from expression and sequence homology alone, independent of every transferred quantity. A nucleus with no cross-species neighbor carrying the quantity received no value. Transferred values are averages over neighbors, so they vary more smoothly than the source values; they were used without re-ranking or rescaling.
From mouse to human, we transferred cell identity (categorical: decidual cell or endometrial fibroblast) and pseudotime (continuous; mouse pseudotime as rank / N). Every mouse nucleus carries a label, whereas the 907 apoptotic decidual nuclei carry no pseudotime. As a result, 47,509 of 47,533 human nuclei (99.95%) received a label and 47,352 (99.6%) a pseudotime value. Transferred labels agreed with the human annotation for 84.2% of nuclei (81.3% of decidual cells and 87.0% of endometrial fibroblasts). Agreement of transferred with human pseudotime (Spearman correlation on ranks) was 0.77 over all nuclei, 0.73 within decidual cells and 0.30 within endometrial fibroblasts.
To test whether the transferred gradient could arise from the SAMap graph or the averaging alone, mouse pseudotime values were permuted among mouse nuclei before transfer, with the graph and edge weights held fixed, either across all 42,290 mouse nuclei with a pseudotime value or only among nuclei of the same mouse compartment (fibroblast or decidual); the latter preserves the difference between compartments but removes the order within them (1,000 permutations each). For each permutation, the Spearman correlation between transferred and human pseudotime was computed over all nuclei and within each compartment, together with the spatial autocorrelation of transferred values (Moran’s I with row-standardized weights on the 10 nearest spatial neighbors within each 10x lane, averaged over the six lanes), over all nuclei and within each compartment with neighbors drawn from the same compartment. Because neighboring human nuclei share mouse neighbors, transferred values are not independent across nuclei, so observed statistics were compared with the permutation distributions rather than with parametric p-values. Permutation across all mouse nuclei removed the gradient (maximum ρ, 0.20; maximum Moran’s I, 0.08). Permutation within compartments retained most of the agreement over all nuclei (means over permutations: ρ = 0.58 vs 0.77 observed) but not within decidual cells (ρ = 0.10 vs 0.73; Moran’s I = 0.21 vs 0.53; no permutation reached the observed values). Within endometrial fibroblasts, most of the agreement remained (ρ = 0.18 vs 0.30; 3 of 1,000 permutations at or above the observed value), so it is largely explained by the fibroblast/decidual split rather than by the order of mouse pseudotime (Supplementary Fig. S31).
From human to mouse, we transferred the human Leiden cluster at resolution 0.5 (categorical; reported as the share of neighbor weight in cluster 3 and as the cluster with the largest share) and each human nucleus’s contrastive cosine to each of the 11 stromal subtypes of Ulrich et al. 2024 (continuous; see [atlas comparison section]). The source nuclei were all 47,533 human fibroblast/decidual nuclei in the SAMap graph, which was fitted before any nucleus was flagged as an embedding outlier; flagged nuclei were kept as sources. 43,196 of 43,197 mouse nuclei received values; one had no human neighbor. The permutation control above applies to the mouse-to-human pseudotime transfer only. For the human-to-mouse transfer, the five perivascular and smooth-muscle subtypes of Ulrich et al. (Stro-1, -2, -3, -4 and -6), which cannot occur in fibroblast/decidual tissue, serve as controls.
Gene regulatory network inference (SCENIC)
Regulons and their per-nucleus activities were inferred with pySCENIC (version 0.12.1; ctxcore 0.2.0, arboreto 0.1.6; Aibar et al. 2017) following the SCENIC protocol (Van de Sande et al. 2020), with the departures noted below, run independently for mouse and human on all fibroblast/decidual nuclei (43,197 mouse, including the 907 apoptotic decidual nuclei, and 47,533 human). As in the protocol, the input was the raw count matrix, without normalization, log-transformation or variable-gene selection. Genes detected in fewer than 1% of nuclei were removed (from 33,698 to 14,245 genes in mouse and from 36,601 to 15,011 in human), leaving 1,179 (mouse) and 1,308 (human) candidate transcription factors from the pySCENIC allTFs lists. The same filtered genes were used for network inference, motif pruning and activity scoring. To quantify regulon reproducibility under both the stochasticity of network inference and the choice of nuclei used to fit it, inference was repeated 100 times per species (seeds 1000–1099), as described in the protocol. Each run drew its own geometric sketch of 15,000 nuclei (geosketch 1.3; Hie et al. 2019) from the scVI latent space of the full object and inferred TF–target co-expression links on that subsample with GRNBoost2 (Moerman et al. 2019). Subsampling was intended: because each run is fit to different nuclei, a regulon must recur across subsamples as well as across seeds to pass the recurrence thresholds below, whereas fitting all nuclei in every run would test only the stochasticity of the inference. Modules were built with the pySCENIC defaults (correlations computed without dropout masking; activating modules only) and pruned with cisTarget against the v10 clustered motif collection (mm10 and hg38 genes_vs_motifs ranking databases for the 500 bp upstream/100 bp downstream and ±10 kb windows, with the corresponding motifs-v10nr_clust-nr annotations). For each motif enriched in a module (AUC threshold 0.05, NES threshold 3.0) and annotated to the module’s transcription factor, the targets ranked highest for that motif were retained (rank threshold 1,500, the default of the pySCENIC Python interface used here; the protocol’s command-line default is 5,000).
Consensus regulons were assembled across the 100 runs. A transcription factor was retained if it formed a regulon in at least 80 of the runs (193 of 771 factors with at least one regulon in mouse; 141 of 941 in human). Within each retained regulon a target was kept if its TF–target link recurred in more than five runs (at least six), the floor applied in the Fly Cell Atlas (Li et al. 2022a). Kept targets were weighted by their occurrence frequency among the runs that recovered the factor instead of being treated as binary members. Regulons with fewer than 10 targets would have been discarded; none were (median 294 targets in mouse, 194 in human). Per-nucleus regulon activity was scored with AUCell in weighted mode on all nuclei of the unsketched object, ranking the same filtered genes, with the pySCENIC default AUC threshold of 0.05 (the top 712 of 14,245 ranked genes in mouse and 751 of 15,011 in human; 9.8% and 5.6% of nuclei, respectively, detected fewer ranked genes than this cutoff) and a fixed random seed (0) for ranking ties.
Regulon activities were related to pseudotime by Spearman correlation with the rank-normalized pseudotime described above; the 907 apoptotic mouse decidual nuclei, which have no pseudotime, were excluded (42,290 mouse and 47,533 human nuclei). Transcription factors were paired across species with the Ensembl 116 one-to-one ortholog table used for the mutual-information comparison: 190 of the 193 mouse and 136 of the 141 human regulon factors have a one-to-one partner, and 111 factors have a regulon in both species. A regulon was considered trajectory-associated if |ρ| ≥ 0.2. Cross-species sign agreement among factors trajectory-associated in both species was tested by permuting the mouse ρ values across the 111 pairs (20,000 permutations). Because some regulons share many targets, the test was repeated counting overlapping regulons once (target-set Jaccard index ≥ 0.3 in either species), with the retained regulon chosen at random (300 repetitions; medians reported).
Decidual zone module scoring
Gene sets for the eleven uterine microenvironments defined by Li et al. (2022b) at pregnancy day 7.5 were taken verbatim from their Table 1, which lists the top 20 marker genes per microenvironment, ranked by fold change and identified with Seurat FindAllMarkers on Harmony-integrated Visium profiles of four D7.5 uterine cross sections. The environments of focus were the primary, transitional, and secondary decidual zones (PDZ, TDZ, SDZ), vascular sinus zone (VSZ), mesometrial decidua (MD), and undifferentiated stroma (udStr). Each set was scored per nucleus with AUCell (pySCENIC 0.12.1) on raw counts, as for the regulon activities, with the default AUC threshold of 0.05 and a fixed random seed (0), which sets the order in which genes with equal counts are ranked; because AUCell ranks genes within each nucleus, normalizing or log-transforming the counts would not change the scores. As for the regulon activities, genes detected in fewer than 1% of the nuclei of the whole-tissue object (570 of 57,015) were not ranked, except that Table 1 genes below this threshold were kept if they were detected in at least one nucleus; without this exception the filter would remove most markers of minority populations (for example 15 of the 20 MD genes). In total 14,903 genes were ranked, so the AUC was computed over the top 745 genes of each nucleus’s ranking, and 9.5% of the scored nuclei detected fewer genes than this cutoff. The 12,324 nuclei of the 3.5 dpi section were scored. For the six modules of focus, scores computed with seeds 0 and 1 agreed closely (Spearman ρ = 0.935–0.989 across these nuclei). In spatial plots each module’s score is displayed from 0 to its 99th percentile among the plotted nuclei of that section. Heatmaps show the mean score per fibroblast/decidual subtype on the same scale.
Comparison with in vitro and in vivo decidualization models
Human in vitro decidualization consensus. We used three studies that profiled human endometrial stromal fibroblasts (ESF) before and after in vitro decidualization and published gene-level differential expression results with directions (Supplementary Table S9). From each study we used every published comparison of cells given the decidualizing stimulus with the same cells without it, at every reported timepoint and treatment arm, with the study's own significance thresholds. Comparisons between donor groups or between decidualization timepoints were not used. From Aghajanova et al. (2011; primary ESF, E2 + P4 versus E2 alone at 6 h, 48 h and 14 days) we used the lists for women without endometriosis (Tables 4 and 5; 179 genes), and from Rytkönen et al. (2019; immortalized ESF, 8-Br-cAMP + MPA versus untreated at 3 and 8 days) the four lists of up- and downregulated genes (Supplemental Table S1A-D; 4,672 genes). Stadtmauer and Wagner (2022; immortalized ESF, MPA + PGE2 or MPA + 8-Br-cAMP versus base medium for 6 days) report per-gene values only for 30 genes chosen as markers of decidual identity, phenotype and senescence (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). A treatment was counted as changing a tabulated gene only where it met the authors' criteria (FDR < 10−6, fold change > 1.5 and > 3 TPM in either condition), which 26 genes did. For 37 further genes, directions were transcribed from the Results text, which assigns named genes to k-means clusters of the genes differentially expressed under at least one treatment and describes each cluster's response to the decidualizing treatments. Each gene was given its cluster's direction (63 genes in total).
Combining the lists. Within each study, a gene's calls across timepoints or treatment arms were collapsed to one direction if they agreed. Genes with conflicting calls were excluded from that study's vote (45 in Rytkönen et al., none in the other two). A gene entered the consensus if at least two studies called it and all calls agreed. Of 147 genes called by two or more studies, 123 agreed (93 up, 30 down) and 24 conflicted. The 123 comprise 77 genes supported by Aghajanova and Rytkönen, 39 by Rytkönen and Stadtmauer (a single cell line) and 7 by all three (ABI3BP, FOXO1, HAND2, IGFBP1, OSR2, PRL, ZBTB16); because the Rytkönen list is far larger than the others, every consensus gene is supported by it. Contributions by study are summarized in Supplementary Table S9 and each gene's calls in Supplementary Table S10. The list was fixed before any comparison with X-Mens, with one later correction: the tabulated Stadtmauer and Wagner genes had first been given directions from the sign of their expression change relative to base medium, without the authors' significance criteria; applying those criteria removed KLF4 and TSHZ1, added ADAMTS5 and CLU, and changed the X-Mens comparison from 19 of 49 to 20 of 50 concordant genes.
Mouse orthologs. Consensus symbols were resolved to current gene symbols and Ensembl identifiers (8 had been renamed, for example C10orf10 to DEPP1), and human-to-mouse orthology was taken from Ensembl 116 orthology components. Only one-to-one orthologs were retained (114 of 123). Genes with no mouse ortholog or with one-to-many or many-to-many relationships were excluded rather than resolved by symbol.
Differential expression in X-Mens. Raw UMI counts of decidual nuclei (all decidual subtypes) and endometrial fibroblast nuclei (all endometrial fibroblast subtypes) were summed per timepoint and cell state across the five induced timepoints (1.5 dpi early, 1.5 dpi late, 2.5 dpi, 3.5 dpi, 4.5 dpi bleeding) and, for fibroblasts only, the uninduced sample, giving 11 pseudobulks from 43,133 nuclei (22,855 decidual and 20,278 fibroblast; 1,170 to 9,270 per pseudobulk, minimum 20). The two pseudobulks at each induced timepoint come from the same samples, so timepoints act as paired replicates. The uninduced sample contributes fibroblasts only (its 64 nuclei labelled decidual were excluded). Because timepoint is in the model, this pseudobulk does not enter the decidual-versus-fibroblast estimate directly; it enters the size-factor normalization and the expression filter. Genes reaching CPM ≥ 1 in at least half of the pseudobulks of either state were retained (16,784). Differential expression was estimated with PyDESeq2 v0.5.4 (Muzellec et al. 2023), a Python implementation of DESeq2 (Love et al. 2014), using Wald tests under the model ~ timepoint + state (4 residual degrees of freedom), and genes were called at q < 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg) and |log2 fold change| > 1.
Differential expression in human endometrium. Raw UMI counts of decidual and fibroblast nuclei were summed per donor and cell type (two donors, one in the menstrual and one in the secretory phase), giving 4 pseudobulks from 47,533 nuclei (23,108 decidual and 24,425 fibroblast; 5,737 to 17,704 per pseudobulk, minimum 20). Genes reaching CPM ≥ 1 in at least half of the pseudobulks of either cell type (that is, in either donor) were retained (20,211). Differential expression was estimated with PyDESeq2 as for X-Mens, using Wald tests under the model ~ donor + cell type (1 residual degree of freedom), and genes were called at q < 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg) and |log2 fold change| > 1 (3,198 genes: 1,867 higher in decidual and 1,331 higher in fibroblast nuclei). With only two donors, dispersion estimates rely largely on the trend across genes, so the q-values alone do not show that a difference replicates across individuals; per-donor log2 CPM ratios and detection rates are therefore reported alongside the pooled estimates.
Scoring and expected agreement. Each of the 114 orthologs was counted as concordant if X-Mens called it in the same direction as in vitro (decidual relative to fibroblast), discordant if in the opposite direction, and not called otherwise. As the agreement expected between unrelated lists, here and below, we used pup(A)·pup(B) + pdown(A)·pdown(B), where pup and pdown are each list's fractions of up- and downregulated genes among the genes compared. X-Mens called 50 orthologs, 20 concordant and 30 discordant (40%; 46% expected by chance); 53 were tested but not called and 11 were not testable (below the expression filter or absent from the feature set).
Comparison with mouse in vivo and in vitro models (Wang et al. 2020b). We used Wang et al.'s published lists of differentially expressed genes for natural pregnancy (implantation versus inter-implantation sites on day 8; 5,277 genes), oil-induced artificial decidualization (oil-stimulated versus unstimulated horn on day 8 of pseudopregnancy; 4,294 genes) and in vitro decidualization (stromal cells from day-4 pseudopregnant uteri treated with E2 + P4 for 4 days, versus control; 1,501 genes). Genes were matched to X-Mens by current mouse symbol, with renamed symbols in the in vitro list resolved through the X-Mens feature manifest (1,446 of 1,501 matched). For each list we computed the fraction of genes called in X-Mens that changed in the same direction, and the same statistic between pairs of Wang lists over their shared genes. X-Mens agreed with oil-induced decidualization for 1,439 of 1,588 genes (91%; 50% expected), with natural pregnancy for 1,105 of 1,518 (73%; 53%) and with in vitro decidualization for 461 of 657 (70%; 53%). Within Wang et al., in vitro decidualization agreed with natural pregnancy for 310 of 766 genes (40%; 44%) and with oil-induced decidualization for 531 of 768 (69%; 53%), and oil-induced decidualization agreed with natural pregnancy for 1,977 of 2,194 (90%; 50%). Gene-level tables are provided in Supplementary Table S11.
The human lists and the Wang in vitro list come from cultured cells and the other Wang lists from whole uterine tissue, and all report direction only, whereas X-Mens contrasts two stromal cell states within the same tissue. Agreement is therefore a direction-level comparison across systems and tissue compositions. X-Mens fibroblasts are progesterone-primed, whereas the reference cells in the human in vitro studies are untreated. Co-regulated genes are counted individually, so a single large program can dominate a percentage, and the percentages are descriptive, so no correction for multiple comparisons was applied.
Reproducibility, code and data availability
The analysis is organized as a Snakemake workflow (Mölder et al. 2021). The real-time-informed pseudotime (symmetrized transition matrix, diffusion map and DPT) adapts code from the CellRank 2 reproducibility repository (Weiler et al. 2024; https://github.com/theislab/cellrank2_reproducibility, notebooks/realtime_kernel/mef/realtime_informed_pseudotime.ipynb, commit 8020e79). scVI models were trained on NVIDIA A40 and A100 GPUs (CUDA 12.9). Differential expression in X-Mens was run with PyDESeq2 0.5.4 under Python 3.11.11 and NumPy 2.2.0; with NumPy 2.4.6, the same genes were called but some p-values differed. Human differential expression was run with PyDESeq2 0.5.4 under Python 3.11.11 and NumPy 2.2.0. Mouse raw sequencing data and processed .h5ad objects are deposited at GEO (accession [X]). Human data are deposited at Harvard Dataverse ([DOI]). The processed objects of both species contain the raw UMI counts of every annotated nucleus (57,015 mouse and 67,792 human) with its sample, timepoint, cell type and subtype, embedding-outlier flag and spatial coordinates; the fibroblast/decidual objects also contain the pseudotime. Extended versions of the all-cell objects (58,710 mouse and 67,868 human nuclei) also contain the four populations set aside during annotation (mouse lymphatic endothelial cells, pericytes and plasma cells; human B cells), labeled by cell type; the obs column in_primary marks the nuclei of the primary objects.
