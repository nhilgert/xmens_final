Computational methods
Alignment, quantification and ambient RNA removal
Raw reads were demultiplexed with Cell Ranger mkfastq (8.0.1) and aligned and quantified with
the 10x Genomics Cell Ranger count pipeline (version 7.2.0) with default parameters. Human
libraries were aligned to the 10x GRCh38 2024-A reference transcriptome. Mouse libraries were
aligned to a custom reference built with Cell Ranger mkref (7.2.0) from the GRCm38 (mm10)
genome and GENCODE vM23 (Ensembl 98) annotation, augmented with the DREADD
transgene sequences (Gq-DREADD and Gs-DREADD). Ambient RNA was removed from the
raw count matrices with CellBender remove-background (Fleming et al. 2023; version 0.2.2;
default 150 epochs, CUDA enabled) per lane. Slide-tags spatial barcodes were processed with
the Slide-tags pipeline (Russell et al. 2024).
Quality control
Unless otherwise noted, quality control and preprocessing used Scanpy (version 1.11.5; Wolf et
al. 2018) and anndata (0.12.11; Virshup et al. 2024). Per sample, nuclei with fewer than 500
CellBender-corrected UMIs were excluded. Following the single-cell best practice
recommendations (Heumos et al. 2023), nuclei in which mitochondrial genes contributed more
than 8% of counts were removed, as were nuclei whose log10-transformed total counts or
log10-transformed number of detected genes lay more than 3 median absolute deviations from
the sample median (Supplementary Table S[X] lists the resulting per-sample cutoffs).
Doublets were identified per sample with DoubletDetection (version 4.2; Gayoso et al. 2018),
which performs Leiden clustering and selection of 3,000 highly variable genes internally. The
boosting classifier was run for 40 iterations with a p-value threshold of 10
−7 and a voter
threshold of 0.5, convergence of the doublet count across iterations was inspected, and
predicted doublets were removed. A second, cluster-level exclusion applied during annotation is
described below. Of 90,456 mouse and 97,470 human CellBender-called nuclei, the UMI floor
removed 7,460 and 4,948, the mitochondrial filter 0 and 7, the count-depth MAD filter 2,211 and
5,474, the detected-gene MAD filter 1,219 and 1,731, and the doublet classifier 11,497 and
14,947, leaving 68,069 mouse and 70,363 human nuclei for integration (filters applied in that
order; per-sample counts in Supplementary Table S[X]).
Dimensionality reduction, clustering and annotation
Samples of each species were concatenated into one AnnData object per species. For each
object, 3,000 highly variable genes were selected with scanpy.pp.highly_variable_genes
(flavor seurat_v3, on raw counts, sample as batch key) after removing mitochondrial and
ribosomal genes from the candidate pool. An scVI variational autoencoder (Lopez et al. 2018;
scvi-tools 1.4.2, PyTorch 2.11.0) was trained with two encoder/decoder layers, 30 latent
dimensions and a negative-binomial likelihood on the raw counts of these genes with sample as
batch key, for the default number of epochs with early stopping and the evidence lower bound
monitored for convergence (seed 0).
A k-nearest-neighbor graph (k = 100) was built on the 30-dimensional latent space. Leiden
clustering (Traag et al. 2019; scanpy.tl.leiden with the igraph implementation, Csardi and
1
Nepusz 2006; random seed 0) was run at 21 resolutions from 0.1 to 10, and a UMAP (McInnes
et al. 2018; scanpy.tl.umap; min_dist 0.5, spread 1.0) was computed on the same graph.
Major cell classes were annotated by manual curation at Leiden resolution 2.5, deliberately
over-clustering relative to the expected number of classes so that populations co-expressing
markers of more than one class resolved as discrete clusters (38 clusters for 6 classes in
mouse, 34 for 5 in human). Each cluster was assigned to a class (stromal, epithelial, immune,
endothelial, smooth muscle and, in mouse only, perimetrial) from canonical marker genes of
published mouse and human endometrial single-cell datasets (Kirkwood et al. 2021, 2022;
Wang et al. 2020a; Winkler et al. 2024), examined with scanpy.pl.dotplot. Clusters with mixed
identity were excluded upon manual marker review (9 of 38 clusters, 9,715 nuclei, in mouse,
leaving 58,354; 3 of 34 clusters, 1,217 nuclei, in human, leaving 69,146). Although identified by
marker review rather than by a doublet threshold, the excluded clusters were enriched for
doublet signal even though all nuclei had already passed the per-nucleus doublet filter (mean
DoubletDetection score 4.66 versus 1.60 in retained nuclei in mouse; 10.49 versus 1.51 in
human).
Retained nuclei were split by major cell class, and each was re-embedded and integrated with
the same scVI procedure (3,000 highly variable genes re-selected on the subset with the
seurat_v3 method, after removing genes detected in fewer than 3 nuclei; the selection pooled
all samples rather than fitting per sample, because per-sample variance fits are unstable in
small classes). Within each class, a 100-nearest-neighbor graph was built on the scVI latent
space and clustered at Leiden resolution 3.0, again deliberately over-clustering relative to the
expected number of cell types (178 clusters for 23 fine cell types in mouse, 179 for 16 in human;
Supplementary Table S[X]). Clusters were merged into fine cell types or excluded on marker
review. The 13 clusters excluded in each species removed a further 1,321 mouse and 2,316
human nuclei, leaving 57,033 mouse and 66,830 human annotated nuclei.
Finally, nuclei were re-integrated using scVI. For UMAP visualization, a handful of cells whose
neighborhoods were inconsistent with their lineage label (at most 20% of 30 nearest neighbors
sharing the nucleus’s lineage and at least 70% from one other lineage, in either the scVI latent
space or the UMAP) were flagged as embedding outliers, which were omitted from UMAP plots
but retained for all other analyses: 34 of 57,033 mouse nuclei (21 of 43,197 fibroblast/decidual)
and 25 of 66,830 human nuclei (7 of 47,533 fibroblast/decidual).
Spatial quality control
Spatial coordinates were quality-controlled after annotation. Positions failing a criterion were set
to missing, though the nuclei themselves were retained for all non-spatial analyses. First, within
each 10x lane, a position was blanked (set to NaN) if it duplicated another nucleus’s coordinate
or if its mean distance to its five nearest positioned neighbors was below 10 µm. Second, the
mean distance to the five nearest neighbors was recomputed among the remaining positions of
each timepoint (lanes pooled), and a position was blanked if log(1 + distance) exceeded the
timepoint median by more than 5 median absolute deviations (unscaled). Third, positions
outside the tissue sections that the distance criterion missed were blanked with ten rectangular
exclusion regions drawn by hand on plots of the raw coordinates (5 in the uninduced sample, 3
at 3 dpi, and 1 each at 1 dpi early and 2 dpi; listed in the workflow configuration). In mouse, the
2
three steps blanked 1,272, 544 and 136 positions, respectively (the exclusion regions covered
112 nuclei in the uninduced sample, 12 at 3 dpi, 7 at 1 dpi early and 5 at 2 dpi), leaving 29,678
of 57,033 nuclei with a position. A further 25,403 had no position from Slide-tags. For the human
data, only the first step was applied (4 positions blanked). In total, 45,730 of 66,830 human
nuclei have a position, and 21,096 had none from Slide-tags. In all spatial figures, nuclei from
10x lanes of one timepoint (sections of the same tissue in one coordinate frame) are pooled,
and mouse sections are rotated rigidly about the median position of their plotted nuclei for
display.
Pseudotime
We ordered endometrial fibroblasts and decidual cells by diffusion pseudotime (DPT) computed
on a time-informed transition matrix, using one procedure for both species. In mouse, the 907
apoptotic decidual nuclei were excluded from the pseudotime computation and carry no
pseudotime value. Timepoints were encoded as days (mouse: uninduced 0, 1 dpi early 1, 1 dpi
late 1.5, 2 dpi 2, 3 dpi 3, 4 dpi 4; human: secretory 0, menstrual 1). Transitions between
consecutive timepoints were estimated by unbalanced entropic optimal transport with moscot
(v0.5.0; Klein et al. 2025; TemporalProblem): squared Euclidean cost in each species'
30-dimensional stromal scVI latent space, scaled by its mean; ε = 10
−3
; τa = τb = 0.95; prior
growth rates from moscot's proliferation and apoptosis gene sets. All couplings converged. The
couplings were combined in a CellRank RealTimeKernel (v2.0.7; Weiler et al. 2024) with the
kernel settings of the CellRank protocol (Weiler and Theis 2026, Procedure 4; self_transitions
= "all", conn_weight = 0.2, threshold = "auto"). Each row thus assigns 80% of its probability
to the nucleus's row-normalized coupling to the next timepoint and 20% to a within-timepoint
15-nearest-neighbor graph (Scanpy defaults: 50-component PCA of log-normalized expression
of the 3,000 stromal highly variable genes, computed per timepoint). Nuclei at the last timepoint
use the within-timepoint graph only. We symmetrized the transition matrix ((T + T
T
)/2, rows
renormalized) and computed a 15-component diffusion map (Coifman et al. 2005). The root was
the earliest-timepoint fibroblast at the fibroblast end of DC1, the component most correlated with
the annotated fibroblast-to-decidual order (Spearman ρ = 0.89 mouse, 0.72 human): an
uninduced eFib1 nucleus in mouse (ACCCTTGAGCATGAAT-1-uninduced_mck8b) and a
secretory-phase fibroblast in human (GAGACCCAGAATAGTC-1-secretory_mck6b). DPT
(Haghverdi et al. 2016) was computed with scanpy.tl.dpt on all 15 components and
rank-normalized (average rank / N) for all downstream analyses. Mouse pseudotime increased
with subtype order (ρ = 0.86) and timepoint (ρ = 0.62). Human fibroblasts preceded decidual
cells (median 0.28 vs 0.75; ρ = 0.70), and pseudotime increased from the secretory to the
menstrual phase (ρ = 0.34).
Pseudotime-associated genes and cross-species conservation
Genes associated with pseudotime were identified with a mutual information (MI)-based
approach adapted from previous studies of the menstrual and mouse estrous cycles (Wang et
al. 2020a; Winkler et al. 2024). All genes detected in at least 1% of nuclei were tested (14,261
mouse and 15,008 human genes). For each such gene, MI between log-normalized expression
and pseudotime was estimated on all nuclei with the k-nearest-neighbor estimator implemented
in sklearn.feature_selection.mutual_info_regression (scikit-learn 1.8.0, Pedregosa et
3
al. 2011; Kraskov et al. 2004). Significance was assessed by permuting pseudotime 1,000
times. Genes were processed in 200 chunks, each tested against the same 1,000 permutations,
and the one-sided empirical p-value was (number of permutations with MI ≥ observed +
1)/(1,000 + 1). P-values were adjusted with the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (Benjamini and
Hochberg 1995; statsmodels 0.14.6), and genes with q < 0.05 were considered significant
(14,261 mouse and 14,913 human genes). Given the permutation resolution and the pervasive
association of expression with differentiation pseudotime, significance served as a floor
excluding estimator noise rather than a strict selective criterion. Top pseudotime-associated
genes were defined as those whose MI exceeded the 90th percentile among significant genes
in each species: 1,426 mouse genes (MI > 0.072 nats) and 1,492 human genes (MI > 0.027
nats) (Supplementary Fig. ??).
Strict one-to-one human-mouse orthologs were obtained from the Ensembl Compara release
116 human-mouse homologies of protein-coding genes (Harrison et al. 2024; homology type
ortholog_one2one; 16,275 gene pairs). The background for the comparison comprised the
9,592 one-to-one pairs in which both genes were detected in at least 1% of fibroblasts and
decidual cells and significant for MI (q < 0.05) in their species, and the top gene sets were
restricted to this background (1,146 mouse genes, K; 1,193 human genes, n). Their overlap (k =
374 pairs) was tested with a hypergeometric test (scipy.stats.hypergeom in SciPy, Virtanen et
al. 2020; N = 9,592, computed on the log scale with hypergeom.logsf): the expected overlap
was K × n / N = 142.5 genes (12% of the human top set) and the observed overlap 31%
(2.62-fold enrichment; p = 1.8 × 10
−84
). To check that the result did not depend on the
90th-percentile threshold, the test was repeated at every percentile cutoff from the 40th to the
99th, recording −log10 p, fold enrichment and the 2.5th-97.5th percentile range of the
hypergeometric null (Supplementary Fig. S16C-D).
Pseudotime expression heatmaps
To compare gene expression changes during induced decidualization to pregnancy in Yang et
al. (2023), nuclei subsetted from induced timepoints were ordered by mouse pseudotime,
re-ranked within this subset. Smoothed profiles of the 28 panel genes were computed with a
reimplementation of Monocle 2's plot_pseudotime_heatmap (Trapnell et al. 2014; Qiu et al.
2017). For each gene, a negative-binomial generalized linear model (VGAM; Yee 2015) of
size-factor-normalized UMI counts was fitted on a natural cubic spline of pseudotime (3 degrees
of freedom). Size factors were library size divided by the geometric mean library size over all
nuclei. Each gene's dispersion was taken from Monocle's parametric dispersion-mean trend
(dispersion = a + b/mean), fitted across all 27,064 genes detected in more than one nucleus.
Fitted values at 100 evenly spaced points along pseudotime (equal numbers of nuclei per
column) were log10(x + 1)-transformed, z-scored per gene and clipped to [−3, 3]. Rows follow
the gene order of Yang et al. (2023) and were not clustered.
For cross-species pseudotime heatmaps, candidate genes were the strict one-to-one orthologs
ranked in the top 10% by MI with pseudotime in both species. For each, we computed the
Pearson correlation between the human and mouse expression profiles along pseudotime
(mean log-normalized expression in 50 equal-count pseudotime bins, z-scored per gene).
Among genes with r > 0.8, genes were classified as early or late by the pseudotime centroid of
4
their profile (< 0.4 or > 0.6), and the 14 most concordant early and 14 most concordant late
genes were displayed (r = 0.88-0.98), ordered by centroid. Smoothed profiles were computed
separately for each species on all fibroblasts and decidual nuclei along that species'
rank-normalized pseudotime, with the Monocle 2 procedure above, then log10(x +
1)-transformed, z-scored per gene within each species and clipped to [−3, 3]. Strips above each
heatmap show the cell-type composition and the most common cell type in each of 100
equal-count pseudotime bins.
Cross-species alignment, cell identity transfer, and pseudotime transfer (SAMap)
Human and mouse proteomes (Ensembl release 113; GRCh38, GRCm39) were searched
reciprocally with phmmer (HMMER 3.4; Eddy 2011) at E ≤ 10
−5
. Because the proteomes include
all annotated isoforms, hits were collapsed to gene symbols using the gene_symbol field of each
FASTA header, retaining the highest-scoring isoform pair per ordered symbol pair. Proteins
lacking a gene symbol were excluded.
Fibroblast and decidual nuclei from all timepoints were supplied to SAMap (sc-samap 3.0.1;
Tarashansky et al. 2021) as raw integer counts indexed by gene symbol. Per-species manifolds
were built with SAMap’s internal settings and stitched over the phmmer tables for three
iterations, with 20 cross-species neighbors per nucleus and edge weights rescaled by
cross-species expression correlation. Compartment correspondence is reported as the share of
each nucleus’s cross-species edge weight that lands in each compartment of the other species,
averaged over the nuclei of the source compartment (share landing in the matching
compartment: 88% for human fibroblasts, 82% for human decidual cells, 85% for mouse
fibroblasts and 92% for mouse decidual cells; mean total cross-species edge weight 12.6-13.5
of a maximum of 20 in all four compartments). The corresponding SAMap alignment scores
were 0.58 for both matched compartments and 0.08 and 0.12 off-diagonal.

Gene regulatory network inference (SCENIC)
Regulons and their per-nucleus activities were inferred with pySCENIC (version 0.12.1; ctxcore
0.2.0, arboreto 0.1.6; Aibar et al. 2017) following the SCENIC protocol (Van de Sande et
al. 2020), run independently for mouse and human on the fibroblast/decidual objects (43,197
and 47,533 nuclei). Raw counts were used as input. Genes detected in fewer than 1% of nuclei
were removed (from 33,698 to 14,245 genes in mouse and from 36,601 to 15,011 in human),
leaving 1,179 (mouse) and 1,308 (human) candidate transcription factors from the pySCENIC
allTFs lists. To quantify regulon reproducibility under both the stochasticity of network inference
and the choice of nuclei used to fit it, inference was repeated 100 times per species, as per the
protocol suggestion. Each run drew its own geometric sketch of 15,000 nuclei (geosketch 1.3;
Hie et al. 2019) from the scVI latent space of the full object and inferred TF-target co-expression
modules on that subsample with GRNBoost2 (Moerman et al. 2019). Modules were pruned with
cisTarget against the v10 clustered motif collection (mm10 and hg38 genes_vs_motifs ranking
databases for the 500 bp upstream/100 bp downstream and +/-10 kb windows, with the
corresponding motifs-v10nr_clust-nr annotations; pySCENIC defaults: rank threshold 1,500,
AUC threshold 0.05, NES threshold 3.0), retaining only targets whose regulatory regions were
enriched for the cognate factor’s motif. No normalization, log-transformation or variable-gene
selection was applied to the SCENIC input: GRNBoost2’s tree regression is invariant to the units
of the expression matrix, and AUCell operates on per-nucleus gene rankings. Sketching and
GRNBoost2 are the only stochastic steps.
Consensus regulons were assembled across the 100 runs. A transcription factor was retained if
it formed a regulon in at least 80 of the runs (193 of 771 factors with at least one regulon in
mouse; 141 of 941 in human). Within each retained regulon a target was kept if its TF-target link
recurred in more than five runs, the floor used in the Fly Cell Atlas (Li et al. 2022a). Kept targets
were weighted by their occurrence frequency among the runs that recovered the factor instead
of being treated as binary members. Per-nucleus regulon activity was scored with AUCell in
weighted mode on all nuclei of the unsketched object, with the pySCENIC default AUC
threshold of 0.05. Regulon activities were related to pseudotime by Spearman correlation with
the rank-normalized values, with transcription factors paired across species by the Ensembl 116
one-to-one ortholog table used for the mutual-information comparison (190 of the 193 mouse
regulon factors have a one-to-one human partner).
Decidual zone module scoring
Gene sets for the eleven uterine microenvironments defined by Li et al. (2022b) at pregnancy
day 7.5 were taken verbatim from their Table 1, which lists the top 20 marker genes per
microenvironment, ranked by fold change and identified with Seurat FindAllMarkers on
Harmony-integrated Visium profiles of four D7.5 uterine cross sections. The environments of
focus were the primary, transitional, and secondary decidual zones (PDZ, TDZ, SDZ), vascular
sinus zone (VSZ), mesometrial decidua (MD), and undifferentiated stroma (udStr). Each set was
scored per nucleus with AUCell (pySCENIC implementation) on CP10K, log1p-normalized
6
counts, with the AUC threshold set by pySCENIC’s derive_auc_threshold at the default 5th
percentile. In spatial plots each module’s score is displayed from 0 to its 99th percentile among
the plotted nuclei of that section. Heatmaps show the mean score per fibroblast/decidual
subtype on the same scale.
Comparison with in vitro and in vivo decidualization models
Human in vitro decidualization consensus. We used three studies that profiled human
endometrial stromal fibroblasts (ESF) before and after in vitro decidualization and published
gene-level differential expression results with directions (Supplementary Table SX). From each
study we used every published comparison of cells given the decidualizing stimulus with the
same cells without it, at every reported timepoint and treatment arm, with the study's own
significance thresholds. Comparisons between donor groups or between decidualization
timepoints were not used. From Aghajanova et al. (2011; primary ESF, E2 + P4 versus E2 alone
at 6 h, 48 h and 14 days) we used the lists for women without endometriosis (Tables 4 and 5;
179 genes), and from Rytkönen et al. (2019; immortalized ESF, 8-Br-cAMP + MPA versus
untreated at 3 and 8 days) the four lists of up- and downregulated genes (Supplemental Table
S1A-D; 4,672 genes). Stadtmauer and Wagner (2022; immortalized ESF, MPA + PGE2 or MPA
+ 8-Br-cAMP versus base medium for 6 days) report per-gene values only for 30 genes chosen
as markers of decidual identity, phenotype and senescence (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3).
A treatment was counted as changing a tabulated gene only where it met the authors' criteria
(FDR < 10
−6
, fold change > 1.5 and > 3 TPM in either condition), which 26 genes did. For 37
further genes, directions were transcribed from the Results text, which assigns named genes to
k-means clusters of the genes differentially expressed under at least one treatment and
describes each cluster's response to the decidualizing treatments. Each gene was given its
cluster's direction (63 genes in total).
Combining the lists. Within each study, a gene's calls across timepoints or treatment arms
were collapsed to one direction if they agreed. Genes with conflicting calls were excluded from
that study's vote (45 in Rytkönen et al., none in the other two). A gene entered the consensus if
at least two studies called it and all calls agreed. Of 147 genes called by two or more studies,
123 agreed (93 up, 30 down) and 24 conflicted. The 123 comprise 77 genes supported by
Aghajanova and Rytkönen, 39 by Rytkönen and Stadtmauer (a single cell line) and 7 by all
three (ABI3BP, FOXO1, HAND2, IGFBP1, OSR2, PRL, ZBTB16); because the Rytkönen list is
far larger than the others, every consensus gene is supported by it. Contributions by study are
summarized in Supplementary Table SX and each gene's calls in Supplementary Table SY. The
list was fixed before any comparison with X-Mens, with one later correction: the tabulated
Stadtmauer genes had first been given directions from the sign of their expression change
alone, and applying the authors' criteria exchanged KLF4 and TSHZ1 for ADAMTS5 and CLU,
changing the X-Mens result from 19 of 49 to 20 of 50 concordant genes.
Mouse orthologs. Consensus symbols were resolved to current gene symbols and Ensembl
identifiers (8 had been renamed, for example C10orf10 to DEPP1), and human-to-mouse
orthology was taken from Ensembl 116 orthology components, cross-checked against OMA
(Altenhoff et al. 2024). Only one-to-one orthologs were retained (114 of 123). Genes with no
7
mouse ortholog or with one-to-many or many-to-many relationships were excluded rather than
resolved by symbol.
Differential expression in X-Mens. Raw UMI counts of decidual nuclei (all decidual subtypes)
and endometrial fibroblast nuclei (all endometrial fibroblast subtypes) were summed per
timepoint and cell state across the five induced timepoints (1 dpi early, 1 dpi late, 2 dpi, 3 dpi, 4
dpi bleeding), giving 10 pseudobulks from 33,863 nuclei (22,855 decidual and 11,008 fibroblast;
1,170 to 8,675 per pseudobulk, minimum 25). The two pseudobulks at each timepoint come
from the same samples, so timepoints act as paired replicates. Genes reaching CPM ≥ 1 in at
least half of the pseudobulks of either state were retained (16,525). Differential expression was
estimated with PyDESeq2 v0.5.4 (Muzellec et al. 2023), a Python implementation of DESeq2
(Love et al. 2014), using Wald tests under the model ~ timepoint + state (4 residual degrees
of freedom), and genes were called at Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold
change| > 1.

Comparison with mouse in vivo and in vitro models (Wang et al. 2020b). We used Wang et
al.'s published lists of differentially expressed genes for natural pregnancy (implantation versus
inter-implantation sites on day 8; 5,277 genes), oil-induced artificial decidualization
(oil-stimulated versus unstimulated horn on day 8 of pseudopregnancy; 4,294 genes) and in
vitro decidualization (stromal cells from day-4 pseudopregnant uteri treated with E2 + P4 for 4
days, versus control; 1,501 genes). Genes were matched to X-Mens by current mouse symbol,
with renamed symbols in the in vitro list resolved through the X-Mens feature manifest (1,446 of
1,501 matched). For each list we computed the fraction of genes called in X-Mens that changed
in the same direction, and the same statistic between pairs of Wang lists over their shared
genes. X-Mens agreed with oil-induced decidualization for 1,429 of 1,578 genes (91%; 50%
expected), with natural pregnancy for 1,092 of 1,505 (73%; 53%) and with in vitro
decidualization for 459 of 654 (70%; 53%). Within Wang et al., in vitro decidualization agreed
with natural pregnancy for 310 of 766 genes (40%; 44%) and with oil-induced decidualization for
531 of 768 (69%; 53%), and oil-induced decidualization agreed with natural pregnancy for 1,977
of 2,194 (90%; 50%). Gene-level tables are provided in Supplementary Table SW.
The human lists and the Wang in vitro list come from cultured cells and the other Wang lists
from whole uterine tissue, and all report direction only, whereas X-Mens contrasts two stromal
cell states within the same tissue. Agreement is therefore a direction-level comparison across
systems and tissue compositions. X-Mens fibroblasts are progesterone-primed, whereas the
reference cells in the human in vitro studies are untreated. Co-regulated genes are counted
individually, so a single large program can dominate a percentage, and the percentages are
descriptive, so no correction for multiple comparisons was applied.

Reproducibility, code and data availability
The analysis is organized as a Snakemake workflow (Mölder et al. 2021). scVI models were
trained on NVIDIA A40 and A100 GPUs (CUDA 12.9). Mouse raw sequencing data and
processed .h5ad objects are deposited at GEO (accession [X]). Human data are deposited at
Harvard Dataverse ([DOI]).
